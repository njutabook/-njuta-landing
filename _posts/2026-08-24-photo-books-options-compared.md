I've been obsessed with photos and keepsakes since forever. Growing up, they made me feel close to my family's past in a way that nothing else could; how special is it that you can hold a time capsule of memories you want to relive, or experience for the first time if you weren't there? That's, I firmly believe, one of the most magical ways to experience life, and it's a big part of why I've spent years testing nearly every photo book service out there and feeling true appreciation for what each one has brought me so far.
 
So cue: my obsession with photo books. It all started when I began to gift them to other people in my life. I have always been the resident photographer in my social and familial circles, so naturally it became a little easier to literally make an album / photo book instead of the less appealing option of airdropping them hundreds of photos. Then I realized that I deserved to have my own too, so then my use of photo books and printing services doubled. Over the years, I got very familiar with most of the main players in the photo book service space, and wanted to do a breakdown of what I've learned when using each one for myself.
 
Here's the quick version of this photo book comparison if you're in a hurry, then the full breakdown below:
 
| Service | Best For | Turnaround Time | Effort to Build |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Snapfish | Budget-friendly layflat books | 5+ business days | High; took months, layout tools not intuitive |
| CVS or Target Same-Day | Same-day pickup | Same day | Low; DIY, old-school assembly |
| Chatbooks | Small, recurring books | 5+ business days | Low; quick, pulls from a phone album |
| Artifact Uprising | Premium paper and cover feel | 5+ business days | Medium; easy editor, limited templates |
| njuta | Hands-off, seasonal curation | Automatic each season | Low; minimal work required |
 
## Snapfish: Best for Budget-Friendly Photo Books
 
I was pleasantly surprised by the layflat book options I ended up going with for my wedding albums, pretty solid quality, and almost a third of the cost of other options out there similar to it.
 
**Not great for** ease of building the book. The layout tools were not the most intuitive, and it was almost like there were too many options to choose from, so it took me forever to decide what each page would look like. My wedding albums took me months to make. They ended up great, but because of the labor of it all I ended up giving one each to my mom and mother in law, and then have still to this day never made one for myself.
 
## CVS or Target Same-Day: Best for Instant Photo Prints
 
Printing photos as soon as possible, and assembling them into a photo album old school style, is what these photo album services do better than anyone else on this list. Most other printing options will take at least 5 business days to print and arrive at your doorstep. With CVS & Target you can put in your order and have it ready within hours, pick it up yourself, and the costs are pretty affordable for what you get.
 
**Not great for** quality output. The printers for these services are not always maintained well because these services are a low priority for CVS/Target, so sometimes you end up grabbing your photos and seeing colors be off or random small streaks from a faulty printing wheel. Most photo book tools will warn you if the photo quality is not large enough for quality printing, but these services don't have features like that, so you might see a photo print a lot grainier than you'd expect.
 
## Chatbooks: Best for Small, Recurring Photo Books
 
Cute, small books that are affordable and help build a habit are Chatbooks' whole thing. When I was using them they had a yearly series which I loved using; it was a perfect way for me to print a year in review booklet for my marriage years. I'd assemble what I want to print into an album on my phone, and it was easy to use Chatbooks' tools to pull from that album directly and print my book. The tooling and integrations are pretty intuitive so it's not hard to tap into your camera roll or even Google Photos.
 
**Not great for** one off printing or printing on a habit that's not monthly. Chatbooks has changed up their pricing model and ultimately, it got too confusing for me to understand their credit system to keep my account active with them. It felt like I had to sign up for printing books monthly, which I certainly didn't have the interest or capacity to do. It wasn't clear to me either if I could just print one off books without having to lock into a recurring subscription. I think things have changed since, but I'd already dropped off as a user by then.
 
## Artifact Uprising: Best for Premium Paper and Cover Quality
 
The books literally feel good to hold, and it puts you in the right head space to savor the memories the book holds. The book editor is pretty easy to use, but template options are more limited than other photo book services, for the sake of the book design. It's like the difference between going to a book aisle in a superstore vs going to a historic library that looks like Harry Potter was filmed in it: it positions you to be in awe of the art of the book. It becomes a special occasion book that you'll certainly cherish and proudly display in your home, and make sure has good storage for future years.
 
**Not great for** affordability. A lot of the pricing hooks you by saying "books **starting at** $XX" which typically means that a certain amount of pages/photos are at that price point, but if you add on more pages or photos the cost goes up. Most photo books have this same model but with Artifact Uprising the cost increase is a lot more significant, and so I don't think this option is accessible to folks who want to regularly print books.
 
## njuta: Best for Hands-Off Curation
 
*Okay, elephant in the room here: You may not have heard of njuta because it's extremely new to the space, and, yes, I am a co-founder of it. I will openly admit that I'm throwing this option in here not to say that it's categorized as a "main player" yet, but, because it's a product that I built with all of my power user pain points and preferences in mind. So with all of these caveats aside, here I go.*
 
The main hook is that it takes the typically most tedious part out of the equation for you: picking what photos make it to print. It'll give you a pretty strong first pass based on quality photos in your camera roll, then you can tweak as much as you want. A true "takes minutes" experience for building a book. Paper quality is excellent too, with Italian Fedrigoni 100# uncoated text, and the cover looks more like an interesting fiction book than a standard photo book. ([If you're curious about why I built njuta, read more here](https://njutabook.com/blog/i-never-finished-my-babys-first-year-photo-book/))
 
**Not great for** one off books or custom templates/covers. njuta is currently only available on a seasonal membership basis, with the point of it being a seasonal habit where you can enjoy and appreciate your year as it's happening, not after it's happened. Cover and photo page templates are fixed because we believe we've made the best design choices we can, and want to build an experience where you have minimal work/decision making involved. But if you're someone who cares to be involved in those details, this may not be the option for you.
 
## So, Which Photo Book Service Is Best for You?
 
The good news about this space is that there are a lot of great options, each with real strengths. Hopefully this breakdown saves you some time deciding, and good luck on your pursuit of preserving your memories. Future you will thank you.
