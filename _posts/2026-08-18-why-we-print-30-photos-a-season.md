The average iPhone camera roll grows by a few thousand photos a year. Most of them don't get looked at twice.

That's not a storage problem; it's a curation problem. The photos worth keeping are buried under screenshots, receipts, and fourteen near-identical shots of the same sunset. Sitting down to sort them is a project, and projects get postponed. That's why the family photo book is a tradition that is getting weaker with younger generations with ever growing camera rolls.

## Quality over quantity

There's a good reason why we take so many photos: we want to remember almost everything, and/or we want more content to post on our socials. But the truth is that memories aren't made stronger by having more literal photos. Memories are made stronger through the stories we tell, the slow, unhurried moments we spend actually remembering what we felt that day a few months ago, and why we cared to snap a shot of it.

There's a temptation to preserve and keep everything on our rolls, when in reality, our focus and time can only really appreciate and truly honor just a photos at a time. 

## Why 30 instead of more

When we were making our decision on how many photos we print a season, we reminded ourselves that less can be more. So we chose to print exactly 30 photos per season, not because more wouldn't fit in the book, but because we think that's the right balance of representing that whole season without overwhelming you like your camera roll does. 

A 30-photo book is small enough that curation can be automatic and the result still feels personal. It's a season of your life at a glance: the ordinary Tuesday dinners, the walk you took a hundred times, the in-between moments that never make the highlight reel on your socials, but are the actual fabric of your life.
