The average iPhone camera roll grows by a few thousand photos a year. Almost none of them get looked at twice.

That's not a storage problem — it's a curation problem. The photos worth keeping are buried under screenshots, receipts, and fourteen near-identical shots of the same sunset. Sitting down to sort them is a project, and projects get postponed. That's why the family photo book is a tradition that mostly died with film.

## The number that makes a book get made

We print 30 photos per season. Not because more wouldn't fit — because more wouldn't get made.

A 30-photo book is small enough that curation can be automatic and the result still feels personal. It's a season of your life at a glance: the ordinary Tuesday dinners, the walk you took a hundred times, the in-between moments that never make the highlight reel but are the actual texture of your life.

## Found for you, on your phone

njuta scans your camera roll on your device — photos never leave your phone during curation — and surfaces the 30 that tell the season's story. You can swap any of them. Then we print it and mail it to you. Four books a year, no project required.

By the time the next season's book arrives, the last one is on your coffee table, getting picked up by guests, doing what photos were always supposed to do: getting looked at.
