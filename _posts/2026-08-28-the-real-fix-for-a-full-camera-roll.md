I have paid to upgrade my iCloud storage more times than I want to admit here. It's pretty crazy to think that I at one point was paying the equivalent of a streaming subscription for simply hoarding my photos with no specific plan on what to do with them. 

I'd keep telling myself that I was going to go through my camera roll to clean out all the duplicates, the bad photos, to at least bring my storage back to a reasonable state. And of course, aspirationally, that I'd figure out some kind of system to print and curate my thousands of photos. Then I became a parent, and obviously those plans went completely out the window. 

## It was never really about storage

The upgrade button is an easy tap because it feels like solving something, when it really isn't. The photos don't go anywhere when you buy more room for them, they just keep piling up somewhere I never scroll back through, at a slightly higher monthly rate than before. 

Here's what actually combats constant upgrades: delete duplicates and screenshots, offload old backups to a computer, or move to Google Photos which is a bit more affordable for more storage. All three buy you room. None of them touch the real problem, which is that most of what's sitting in a full camera roll was never going to become anything either way. 

## The advice that never actually helped

The only people who I know who seem above this storage space problem, are people who either a) review and delete photos on a weekly basis, or more often and b) have some type of ritual like cleaning out their camera roll while they're on a flight and have nothing else to do. The challenge with these habits is how manual and time consuming they are; if you're a new parent like me, you know that having time to do something like this is a true luxury we don't really have. 

## What actually brings a camera roll to life

Paying for storage isn't the real problem; the real problem is that your camera roll is sitting there, without the appreciation and curation that it deserves. It's almost like an abandoned storage unit that doesn't get reopened. If you actually made regular use out of your camera roll - beyond passive storage - those dollars would feel more purposeful, and less reactive. 

I admire the people who have a monthly or quarterly or annual ritual of going through their camera rolls and posting a tasteful "dump" of their roll onto social media or hand picking print worthy photos and turning them into a recurring album or photo book. I've been both of these types of people at some points in my life, but no longer have the time to do either. 

So I built [njuta](https://njutabook.com), a low effort way to rescue the best of your camera roll every season. Instead of me having to spend hours of staring at my camera roll and making decisions on what to do, it does the heavy lifting for me and gives me a treat every three months, that make me appreciate my camera roll (and also, my daily life). My camera roll no longer feels like a burden eating up storage dollars; it now feels like I have a relationship with my camera roll, and we have a quarterly date to catch up on the good stuff. 

## Food for thought

Next time that notification shows up, it's worth noticing what a full camera roll is actually asking. Not "do you want more room," but "what are you going to do with everything already in here." Buying more room doesn't answer that. It just makes the pile easier to ignore for another few months. 

Find a rhythm that feels right for you to rescue your camera roll off of the cloud; then maybe the nest time a storage upgrade alert comes up, you won't have an anxious response to doing it right away. Because maybe you know you've curated and saved the good stuff IRL.
