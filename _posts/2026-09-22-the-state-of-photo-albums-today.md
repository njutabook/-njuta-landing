Growing up, I cherished every photo album in my house: the themed ones that kept our baby milestone keepsakes or special photos from my parents' wedding, the ones that were specifically set aside for my memories (vs my brother's or sister's), even the random ones that had no obvious or clear topic of focus, but contained gems that made me curious and wonder about what stories they held. 

I would flip through them over and over and over again, even if I knew exactly what I'd see next. I loved seeing my parents as younger adults, I loved reliving fuzzy childhood memories I barely remembered, and I loved being able to study every big and small detail by holding the photos in my hands.

When I became a parent, I wanted to be able to recreate that same magic for my kiddo. I knew how much it meant to me, and I wanted it to be a habit that I built into our family norms so that she could grow up knowing how powerful and important physical keepsakes are in honoring cherished memories. But then life happened, and after months and months of feeling TERRIBLE mom guilt about not making my baby's first year photo album, I finally decided to give up to the powers of my photo storage space. 

It made me start to wonder: Where has this household tradition gone? Do people somehow still do it anymore? 

## Grandparents Still Print

Believe it or not: That same generation of parents who were diligent in keeping up with printed photos and album creations, are still maintaining that rhythm for themselves today. Through thick and thin, they deal with the hassle of their camera rolls, muscle through complicated layout tools, and faithfully and consistently print out books of latest travels, prints of their grandkids to stick on their fridge doors, and of course, photo calendars that hold precious gems on each monthly spread. 

They have the discipline, time, and grit to make it through, and are continuing to gift their loved ones and themselves with physical keepsakes that ground them in what's important. 

Let's give up a round of applause for this group of organized, diligent photo keeping folks! 

## Gen Z is Returning to Print

Kodak has reported that disposable and digital camera sales have roughly doubled over the past five years, with Gen Z leading that demand, and one 2025 market report pegged the disposable camera market at over a billion dollars, with Gen Z making up the majority of buyers. This is a surprising fact given that this generation is the first to grow up with the internet & online social platforms from day 1.

You'd think that they would be content with their camera rolls on their phone, but in fact, they're not. They are the first generation to really feel and see how overwhelming digital content can be, and have a true resistance against it all. 

They see the value of print and will spend the money and time to honor key moments in their lives with off device cameras. 

## Millennials are In Between 

As a millennial, I can attest to the confusion that I feel about printing and not printing. While I grew up with the printed keepsakes, I am also aware of just how much work and effort it took to make them come to life. In this day and age, I am simply too busy or distracted on a day to day to commit to time consuming projects like this on a regular basis.

I also enjoy and appreciate the quality of photos that my phone captures, and appreciate the ease of being able to capture content 24/7, as long as my phone is around. The convenience factor keeps me from going to off device options, and also, the cost awareness that disposable cameras and film prints can add up quickly, if unmonitored. I like that my content capture isn't capped at a certain amount of photos or storage space.

So basically, I want the best of both worlds: The routine, rhythm of print across time while also leveraging my phone's camera roll to capture content - all while not overcommitting myself to photo admin with the time I don't have (especially as a millennial parent).
